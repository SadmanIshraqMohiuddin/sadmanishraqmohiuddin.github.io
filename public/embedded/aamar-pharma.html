The Problem

A healthcare logistics platform with no operational infrastructure

The platform had a product concept but no operational backbone. Pharmacy sourcing was ad-hoc, delivery SLAs were undefined, and there was no data infrastructure to understand demand patterns, customer behaviour, or product velocity.

Scaling beyond the initial pilot required building processes, analytics, and a partner network from scratch - simultaneously managing the operation and designing the systems to make it work.

📦

No SLA Framework

Delivery times were untracked and inconsistent. No benchmark existed against which to measure or improve performance.

📊

Zero Data Visibility

No analytics infrastructure. Order patterns, rejection drivers, peak demand hours, and product velocity were unknown.

🏪

Pharmacy Network Bottleneck

Limited to 12 pharmacy partners at pilot. Sourcing failures were the primary cause of rejections and customer churn.

📋

No Operational SOPs

Order intake, prescription verification, dispatch coordination, and last-mile delivery operated without documented standards.

My Role

Operations architect, BA lead, and data analyst - simultaneously

As Head of Operations and Business Analysis Lead, I was responsible for the full operational build - designing workflows, building the analytics infrastructure, managing a team of ~10 staff, and expanding the pharmacy partner network from 12 to 40+.

I also operated as the internal BA lead - translating operational data into platform requirements for the OMS, B2C ordering platform, and B2B pharmacy integrations.

Key Analytical Findings

Four insights that changed how we operated

FINDING 01
7 of 10 top medicines are chronic disease prescriptions
Gastric (Sergel, Pantonix, Seclo), Cardiac (Atova, Ecosprin), Neurological (Rivotril) - all require monthly refills. This directly explained the 70% retention rate and drove the pharmacy pre-stocking strategy for high-velocity chronic Rx items.
FINDING 02
Zone A concentration unlocked the 74-minute SLA
75.2% of all deliveries concentrated in Zone A. Geographic density enabled optimised driver routing and pharmacy partner placement - making the 74-min average delivery not just possible, but sustainable at scale.
FINDING 03
Dual peak hours required a full shift restructure
SQL analysis of 36,802 records revealed two daily peaks: 10AM–1PM (morning dose refills) and 7PM–10PM (evening chronic Rx). Shift scheduling and pharmacy sourcing windows were redesigned around these patterns, reducing dispatch lag and improving SLA compliance.
FINDING 04
Top 3 rejection drivers each had a distinct SOP fix
Root cause analysis identified out-of-stock items, prescription validation issues, and zone coverage gaps as the primary rejection drivers. Each was addressed via a targeted SOP change - reducing the rejection rate over the operational period.
